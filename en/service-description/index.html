Service description

SocialHub CX service specification

SocialHub CX is SocialHub's customer service software for social media, WhatsApp and email. This service specification lists all features by module, security and data protection information and the services not included.

Request documentsRequest a demo

How to use this document

This service specification describes the scope of SocialHub CX as of 20 Oct 2026. Individual items can be used for market research, specifications or evaluation matrices. Numbering stays stable between versions.

1. Workspace and basics

  • LB-1.1 Web-based workspace with two modules: Email and Social; shared ticket logic with status, priority, owner and tags.
  • LB-1.2 Sign-in via the SocialHub account; single sign-on (SSO) available.
  • LB-1.3 Presence status Online/Away; people who are away receive no newly routed tickets.
  • LB-1.4 In-product and browser push notifications.

2. Email module (ticketing)

  • LB-2.1 Connect mailboxes via Microsoft (OAuth) or IMAP/SMTP; manual sync on demand.
  • LB-2.2 Every incoming email becomes a ticket with a ticket number; replies in the thread are assigned to the ticket.
  • LB-2.3 Views including Priority, My tickets, Unassigned, Drafts, Snoozed, Waiting, On hold, Done, Deleted.
  • LB-2.4 Search by ticket number, person or content; filter by channel, tag, priority, status, assignee and date range.
  • LB-2.5 Bulk actions: mark done, assign to me, set priority.
  • LB-2.6 Snooze until a selected time; merge tickets.
  • LB-2.7 Status after sending selectable (e.g. done, on hold, waiting on customer).
  • LB-2.8 Held emails (e.g. bounces, out-of-office replies) with reason; convert into a ticket in one click.
  • LB-2.9 Signatures and automatic acknowledgement with ticket number per mailbox.

3. Social module

  • LB-3.1 Message types as tickets: comments, direct messages, mentions, posts, reviews.
  • LB-3.2 Channels: Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube, LinkedIn, Bluesky, TikTok and Threads, plus Google Business Profile, Google Play Store, Trustpilot and WordPress; WhatsApp as a channel. Some channels as add-ons.
  • LB-3.3 Filter by message type, channel, date range; sorting and search; team scope.
  • LB-3.4 Social dashboard: tickets, handled, response rate, response time, handling time; breakdown by type, channel and sentiment.

4. Replies, templates and knowledge

  • LB-4.1 Rich-text editor with reply templates via “/”, also in internal notes.
  • LB-4.2 Template folders and variables (e.g. first name, ticket number); maintained by admins.
  • LB-4.3 Linking help-centre articles with suggestions matching the ticket.
  • LB-4.4 Knowledge sources: help-centre articles and files, with title and semantic search and a status per source.

5. AI Co-Pilot

  • LB-5.1 Quick actions in email tickets: draft a reply, summarise ticket, ask the knowledge base.
  • LB-5.2 General chat with history; questions in any language.
  • LB-5.3 Sources shown with every answer; articles take precedence over learned tickets.
  • LB-5.4 Human in the loop: staff review and send replies; optionally prepared drafts are never sent automatically.
  • LB-5.5 Optional: learning from solved tickets of the last twelve months as anonymised summaries (off by default).
  • LB-5.6 Feedback with ratings, review queue and CSV export.
  • LB-5.7 SocialHub CX's AI runs on Microsoft Azure with an EU endpoint.

6. Organisation, routing and service level

  • LB-6.1 Rule-based folders (continuously updated) and manual folders (drag & drop), shared account-wide.
  • LB-6.2 Tags; optional AI tagging of new tickets and AI improvement of tag descriptions (reversible).
  • LB-6.3 Teams as views and assignment targets.
  • LB-6.4 Routing per mailbox: keyword rules first, then team, person, round robin or unassigned.
  • LB-6.5 First-response target in business hours (1, 2, 4, 8, 24 or custom).

7. Collaboration and traceability

  • LB-7.1 Internal notes, not visible to customers.
  • LB-7.2 Watchers notified of status and owner changes.
  • LB-7.3 Customer history with all tickets of a person.
  • LB-7.4 Activity log per ticket (notes, assignments, status changes).

8. Reporting

  • LB-8.1 Email dashboard: total, solved, unsolved tickets, median first-response time, late first responses, resolution time, one-touch rate, reopens.
  • LB-8.2 Trends, backlog, arrival times by hour, weekday or month; team and agent performance; Co-Pilot metrics.
  • LB-8.3 Comparison with previous period or year; filter by team and person.

9. Security, data protection and operations

  • LB-9.1 Provider: maloon GmbH (SocialHub), Ingolstadt, Germany.
  • LB-9.2 Hosting in Germany (Falkenstein, Nuremberg) by a provider with a BSI C5 attestation; SocialHub itself does not hold its own C5 attestation.
  • LB-9.3 SocialHub is ISO 27001 certified; regular penetration tests.
  • LB-9.4 Processing under GDPR and BDSG; data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR available.
  • LB-9.5 Data transfer secured via SSL/SSH, VPN tunnels and firewalls.
  • LB-9.6 SocialHub CX's AI runs on Microsoft Azure with an EU endpoint.
  • LB-9.7 SocialHub CX has been tested for accessibility according to BITV. Test information is included in the procurement & IT documents.
  • LB-9.8 SocialHub CX does not train AI models on customer data.

10. Not included

  • Web chat
  • CRM and specialist-system integrations
  • Open API
  • Further SLA types (e.g. resolution targets, escalations) – the first-response target is measured
  • Automatic sending of AI replies

As of: 20 Oct 2026

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

May I use the SocialHub CX service specification in a tender?

Yes, you may use items from the SocialHub CX service specification for market research, specifications or evaluation matrices. In the procurement procedure, make sure to use product-neutral wording. Numbers stay stable, so you can refer to individual items unambiguously in bidder questions.

Is the SocialHub CX service specification available as a PDF?

Yes, the SocialHub CX service specification is print-friendly and included as a PDF in the procurement & IT documents. The online version is always current and dated. When changes occur, the numbers of existing items are kept.

Q&A

Questions and answers

Does the tool bring all channels together in one place, or do I have to switch tools?

SocialHub CX brings email, WhatsApp and social media together in one workspace, so your team doesn't have to switch tools. You handle emails in the Email module and social media messages in the Social module, both as tickets. In the Social module you sort by priority and filter by channel and message type.

Who is SocialHub CX suitable for?

SocialHub CX suits service teams that handle many requests via social media, WhatsApp and email, for example in public transport, retail and loyalty, banking and insurance, public administration and municipal utilities. Typical users are teams that manage several mailboxes and profiles together, want clear ownership and measure first responses.

Does SocialHub CX replace a CRM for a municipal utility's sales, marketing and customer service?

No, SocialHub CX does not replace a CRM; it is customer service software for social media, WhatsApp and email. Utilities use it to handle requests on instalments, bills and outages in service mailboxes and social media profiles. CRM integrations and an open API are not part of the scope; you keep maintaining customer data in your billing or CRM system.

How do we handle requests from Instagram DMs and X replies when email runs elsewhere?

SocialHub CX brings Instagram direct messages, replies on X and emails together in one workspace, so no separate tool is needed. Social media messages land as tickets in the Social module, emails in the Email module. In the Social module you filter by channel, message type and date range; for emails, the Co-Pilot drafts replies from your knowledge base.

Which tools handle not just direct messages but also comments and mentions?

SocialHub CX handles comments, mentions, posts and reviews as tickets in addition to direct messages. This applies to the networks SocialHub connects, including Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube, LinkedIn, TikTok, Google Business and Trustpilot; some of them as add-ons. Your team filters by message type and channel and handles everything in the same workspace as emails.

How do teams handle a very high number of comments per day in one shared inbox?

SocialHub CX helps with high comment volumes in the Social module through filters by message type, channel and date range, search and the “priority first” sort order. The team view shows what is pending for which team. The social dashboard shows tickets, handled tickets, response rate and response time so you can plan shifts. For emails, rule-based folders, templates and bulk actions are added.

How do we manage requests from several social media profiles in one view?

SocialHub CX shows requests from all connected social media profiles in one shared ticket list in the Social module. Filters by channel, message type and date range narrow the view, the “priority first” sort order puts urgent items at the top, and the team view shows all channels or one team. This way your team keeps track even with many profiles, without switching accounts.

How do we bring order to messy customer tickets?

SocialHub CX organises email tickets by status, priority, owner and tags as well as rule-based folders that keep updating. Views such as Priority, My tickets, Waiting and Snoozed show what is pending. Keyword rules per mailbox distribute new emails automatically, and the activity log records who changed what and when.

Must software accessibility also be tested with screen readers and speech input?

SocialHub CX has been tested for accessibility according to BITV. Further accessibility information is available on request. Which evidence and test steps a procurement requires is set by the contracting authority; for public bodies, BITV 2.0 and the European standard EN 301 549 are decisive.

When is a normal email inbox no longer enough for shop support?

SocialHub CX pays off once several people share a mailbox and ownership, duplicate replies or forgotten emails become a problem. Then tickets with status and owner, routing per mailbox, templates and a first-response target help. If requests via social media or WhatsApp come on top, they run in the same workspace.

How many comments and emails per day can a small team handle?

SocialHub CX doesn't give a flat number but makes your team's capacity measurable: the dashboard shows arrivals, backlog, handling time and team performance by hour, weekday or month. Templates, routing and Co-Pilot drafts shorten handling. From your own figures you can derive how many requests your team actually manages.
